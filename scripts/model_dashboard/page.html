Acceptance gate

The green band is the passing range and the black tick is the threshold. If a gated metric fails, the notebook refuses to export the model. Reported metrics are tracked but do not block.

Training data

Every dataset is built from O*NET 31.0, a public occupational taxonomy (CC BY 4.0), and holds no real user data. Point at a bar for its occupations and source.

    Definitions and caveats
    Occupation precision (ML-001)
    The share of generated titles that belong to the input title's O*NET occupation, or to an occupation O*NET lists as closely related ("Primary-Short"). The reference baselines show what random titles from progressively closer occupation groups would score.
    Precision at 0.70 (ML-002)
    Of all title pairs the model scores at 0.70 or above, the share that really are the same or a closely related occupation. 0.70 is the spec's relevance threshold and 0.88 its duplicate threshold.
    Proxy labels
    No human-labelled title-similarity benchmark exists, so ML-002 is evaluated against labels derived from O*NET's occupation structure. Those labels sometimes disagree with what titles mean, which is why Pearson r is reported but no longer gated (D-008).
    Where the latest numbers come from
    The current metrics are read from the manifests inside the bundles on disk. The app's own inspector, findrr model inspect, verified both bundles' SHA-256 checksums on 27 Nov 2026. Earlier runs come from the Kaggle output recorded in the decision log and the project status log. ML-001's run 2 exposed a bug in the decoding constraint that let titles repeat, which PR #31 fixed.
    Memory
    Peak memory is the whole Python process, including the PyTorch or ONNX runtime, not only the model weights. The specs' budgets (300 MB for ML-001, 100 MB for ML-002) describe the model's active RAM, so the two are not directly comparable. A process-level figure this far over budget still deserves a closer look.
    Real outputs
    The samples are a handful of hand-picked inputs, run once through the app's own runners. They illustrate behaviour and are not an evaluation.
